Scope · Build With AI: Basics

Classroom Bluffer (working title)

A multiplayer social deduction game set in a school, where the class's AI study assistant might be lying, and one classmate is the reason why.

status: approved

The unique kernel

The class shares Study-Bot, an AI that always answers confidently. One player, the Bluffer, can secretly make it lie. Every task is a small moment of deciding whether to trust a confident AI or double-check it. Players learn to doubt AI answers by losing to a liar, not by being lectured.

Who it's for the player

Asian Gen Z players, especially in Indonesia, who play Mobile Legends and Roblox with friends and use AI chatbots for search, school, and entertainment. Today they mostly accept AI answers without checking. In the research you cited, up to ~51% accepted plausible wrong answers and only ~25% clicked through to sources.

A friend group that wants a quick game together, each on their own phone.

The core loop, step by step 6 steps · max 10 min
  1. Friends join a game room from their own phones and get secret roles: Students, one Bluffer, and one Fact-Checker.
  2. Players walk around a 2D school map to Study-Bot terminals in different rooms.
  3. At a terminal, a school-subject or trivia question appears. Study-Bot answers confidently. The player trusts it (fast) or double-checks (costs time).
  4. The Bluffer secretly corrupts terminals (with a cooldown) and fakes tasks. The Fact-Checker can verify an answer for certain but has to persuade the class without exposing themselves.
  5. A trusted lie is a strike. Anyone can call a meeting, where players argue about who was where and vote someone out.
  6. Students win by catching the Bluffer or finishing enough correct answers. The Bluffer wins through enough strikes.

Why they come back: the drama of accusing friends, alibis, and the satisfaction of catching the liar.

Inspiration & identity what it should feel like
  • Among Us: walking a map, tasks in rooms, meetings, voting. Also the visual reference: 2D, Among Us–style art and camera.
  • Werewolf: hidden roles, discussion, voting someone out.
  • Roblox Murder Mystery: the Sheriff, which became the Fact-Checker.
  • Kahoot / Among Us room codes: everyone joins from their own phone.
  • Must not feel like an "educational game." The learning is the gameplay.
  • Questions work best when the wrong answer sounds right (common misconceptions).

Why this matters to you the personal stake

You've been reading research that Gen Z's AI use can reduce critical thinking, and that Gen Z plays a lot of games. You want to meet players where they already are, with something that doesn't feel educational:

"if this project successful I think it can be good impact"

You also see a game with real walking as what makes you "a strong contender in this hackathon."

What "working" looks like the finish line

4–5 friends, each on their own phone, join the same game, walk around the 2D school, answer questions at Study-Bot terminals, and get burned or saved by trusting or checking. A meeting is called.

The "oh, that's cool" moment: "The bluffer get caught then kicked out from school."
